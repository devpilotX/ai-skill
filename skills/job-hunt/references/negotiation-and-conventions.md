# Negotiation and conventions

Pay transparency rules, package components, CV conventions by country, and scam red flags. Laws and
conventions in this file change, so retrieve the current position from the named source before relying
on it.

## Pay transparency and salary history rules

Work out which rules apply before advising on who names the first number. The rules usually follow
where the job is performed, and some apply to remote roles that could be filled in the jurisdiction.

United States. There is no general federal posting requirement. States and cities set their own rules
in three forms: a range in the job posting, a range on request or at a set stage, and bans on asking
for salary history. Trackers published in 2026 listed posting-range laws in California, Colorado,
Hawaii, Illinois, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Minnesota, New Jersey, New York, Vermont, Virginia
and Washington, plus the District of Columbia, with Connecticut and Delaware scheduled to follow.
Employer size thresholds and effective dates differ by state. Retrieve the current list from a
maintained tracker such as the
[HR Dive pay transparency law tracker](https://www.hrdive.com/news/pay-transparency-law-tracker-states-that-require-employers-to-post-pay-range-or-wage-range/622542/)
and confirm with the state labour department.

European Union. [Directive (EU) 2023/970](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2023/970/oj/eng), the Pay
Transparency Directive, requires employers to tell candidates the initial pay or its range before the
interview, and bars them from asking about pay history in current or previous employment (Article 5).
Member states had to transpose it by 7 June 2026. Check the national implementing law, since timing
and detail vary by country.

Elsewhere. Retrieve the rules from the national labour ministry or equality body. The UK had no general
posting requirement when this file was written; check whether that has changed.

What changes in the conversation:

- Where a posted range exists, anchor on it and negotiate with evidence for the upper end or above.
- Where salary history questions are banned, the user can say "I'd like to focus on the pay for this role" and move to their researched range.
- Where no rule applies, avoid naming the first number if possible; if pressed, give a sourced range.
- If an employer appears to break a rule, record it and ask an employment lawyer or the enforcing agency.

## Package components

Negotiate the whole package. Several items cost the employer less than base pay.

- Base pay and the review cycle
- Bonus: target, what it depends on, and the historical payout rate
- Equity: instrument, number of units, vesting, strike, and the exercise window (the checklist in `career-strategy` covers valuation)
- Pension or retirement match, and any vesting on the match
- Sign-on bonus to cover forfeited bonus or equity, with its clawback terms
- Holiday and leave
- Notice period and garden leave
- Remote or hybrid arrangement, written into the contract
- Equipment, home office, and training budget
- Relocation and immigration costs
- Start date
- Job title and level, which set the next raise and the next search

## CV conventions by country

These are conventions to check with local sources, such as the national careers service, university
careers offices and recruiters in the sector. They are not rules.

- United States: resume, often one page early in a career and two later. Photos, date of birth and marital status are usually left off.
- United Kingdom: CV, two pages is common. Photos and date of birth are usually left off.
- Germany: Lebenslauf, often tabular and reverse chronological, and traditionally more personal detail than in the US or UK; a photo was long customary and is now optional. Applications may include certificates and references (Arbeitszeugnisse). Check current employer expectations.
- Other EU countries: expectations vary. Some public sector and EU institution applications use the Europass format.
- Academic CVs in any country: long by design, with publications, grants, teaching and talks. Length is not a fault.
- Canada and Australia: resume conventions are closer to the US and UK than to Germany. Check locally.

## Job scam and fake recruiter red flags

The FTC guidance [Looking for a job? Spot the scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2024/07/looking-job-spot-scams)
says honest employers never ask candidates to pay for a job and never send a cheque and ask for part
of it back. Treat any of these as a stop sign until verified:

- A cheque or transfer arrives with instructions to buy equipment, gift cards or software from a named vendor, or to forward part of the money. This is a fake cheque scam; the cheque bounces after the money has gone.
- A request to pay for training, a background check, a visa or equipment.
- Interviews held only by text, chat, WhatsApp or Telegram, with no video call and no company email domain. The FTC notes that unexpected job texts on these apps are a common scam channel.
- An offer made without a real interview, or pay well above the retrieved market range for easy work.
- Requests for a passport scan, national insurance or social security number, or bank details before a written offer from a verified employer. This is how identity theft starts.
- A recruiter email from a personal or look-alike domain, or a role that does not appear on the company's own careers page.
- Pressure to act today, or to keep the process secret.

Verification: find the company's contact details independently, not from the message, and call or email
to confirm the recruiter and the role. If money or documents were already sent, contact the bank at once
and report it to the national fraud reporting body (in the US, ReportFraud.ftc.gov and IdentityTheft.gov;
in the UK, Action Fraud).
